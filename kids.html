Mom’s Big Change

Page 1 of 6. Mom’s body is going through a big change. Grown-ups’ bodies change too, not just kids’ bodies. Hers is changing in a way that takes a few years.

Page 2 of 6. Sometimes Mom gets hot, very hot, all of a sudden. She might open a window or hold up a fan. It’s called a hot flash. It goes away after a little while.

Page 3 of 6. Sometimes Mom doesn’t sleep well. When she’s tired, she might want to sit and rest. That doesn’t mean she doesn’t want to play with you. She is just very sleepy.

Page 4 of 6. Sometimes Mom gets grumpy, or sad for no reason. Her body makes some feelings bigger. It is never, ever because of you. You didn’t do anything wrong.

Page 5 of 6. You can help! You can give a hug. You can say, “Do you need a rest?” You can draw her a picture, or bring her a cup of water. Small things help a lot.

Page 6 of 6. Mom loves you just the same, all the time, even on the hot days and the grumpy days. This big change won’t last forever, and you are not alone. You are here with her.

A note for the grown-up reading this

Kids notice when something shifts at home, and they often decide it must be their fault. A short, calm story can lift that weight.

Read it at a quiet moment, not in the middle of a hard one. Let them ask questions. It’s fine to say “I don’t know” or “I’ll tell you more when you’re older.”

Keep your explanation true and simple. You don’t need to share details about your body or health.

What you might notice

  • Mom feels hot all of a sudden
  • Mom is sleepy and wants to rest
  • Mom gets grumpy or teary sometimes
  • Mom forgets little things

What helps

  • A hug or a drawing
  • Asking “Do you need a rest?”
  • A cup of cool water
  • Using your quiet voice when she’s tired

What doesn’t help

  • Thinking it’s your fault (it’s never your fault)
  • Asking again and again when she’s tired
  • Hiding your feelings from her

Things you can say

  • “I love you, Mom.”
  • “Do you want a hug?”
  • “Is your hot flash over now?”

What not to say

“Why are you always so grumpy?”

Try this instead“Are you having a hard day? I can help.”
